Introduce yourself as an artist 以艺术家的身份介绍自己

Artist statement艺术家自述

Original · 964 characters · 2026原文 · 964 字符 · 2026

I draw picture books for children. I don't tell kids what is right or wrong. I put small signs in my pictures—a shadow, a colour that shifts—so feelings they can't name yet become something they can see.

Most of my pages start in Procreate and Photoshop. I keep my characters soft and my colours quiet. Storyboards, page layout, the look on a face — these carry the comfort. A drooping shoulder or a toy half-hidden behind a chair often shows worry better than words. I turn children's vague feelings into faces, bodies and small details, so readers feel them straight from the picture.

My work sits within children's emotional education. When kids look at my pictures, I want them to find their own feelings there. I don't tell them what to think. I just invite them to notice the messy ones, the mixed ones, and to let those feelings be. I try to create a gentle space where being small and scared isn't something you need to fix; it is okay just to sit with it.

我为孩子画绘本。我不告诉他们什么是对、什么错。我在画里放下一些小小的暗示——一道影子、一处正在变化的颜色——让那些他们还叫不出名字的情绪，变成看得见的东西。

大部分页面从 Procreate 和 Photoshop 开始。我把人物画得柔软，把颜色压得安静。分镜、版面、脸上的表情，这些都承担着那份安稳。垮下去的肩膀、藏在椅子后面的半个玩偶，常常比语言更会说担心。我把孩子模糊的情绪，变成脸、身体和小细节，让读者直接从画面里感觉到。

我的作品属于儿童情绪教育。当孩子看我的画，我希望他们在里面找到自己的情绪。我不告诉他们该怎么想，只是邀请他们去留意那些混乱的、混合的情绪，并允许它们存在。我想创造一个温柔的空间：小小的、害怕的，不是需要被"修好"的东西；只是待在里面，也没关系。

Five commandments I draw by我画画时遵守的五条

It's Okay That I Cried我，哭了没关系

Parent handbook家长手册 2026 6 pages · a guide for parents6 页 · 亲子共读指南

A therapeutic picture book for children aged 3–6 and the adults who read with them. The protagonist is an ordinary girl, not a star: she cries when she falls, gets upset over a toy, and doesn't want to let go at the kindergarten gate. The book never says “don't cry”. It follows one feeling from the moment it appears to the moment it settles, and lets it be normal. The handbook was written for the second reader — the adult who often doesn't know what to say. 一本写给 3–6 岁孩子的情绪疗愈绘本，也写给他身边一起读的大人。主角并不是什么小明星，只是一个普通的小女孩：摔倒了会哭，抢玩具会委屈，在幼儿园门口舍不得松手。这本书从不说“不许哭”，它只是陪一种情绪从出现、到顶点、再到平息，让“这样很正常”被说出来。家长手册则写给第二位读者——那位常常不知道该怎么开口的大人。

What I actually learned 我真正学到的东西

Four things from this course that changed how I look at my own work — and two things you can play with below. 这门课里有四件事改变了我看自己作品的方式——下面还有两个可以动手玩一玩的部分。

01

Media is not neutral媒介并不中立

Digital media theory taught me that software settings, platform rules and algorithms decide part of what we see. I no longer assume my default brushes, or the style that happens to be trending, are simply “how it looks”. If I follow an algorithm's taste without asking, I am not making a choice — I am inheriting one. 数字媒介理论让我意识到：软件的默认设置、平台规则和算法，都在决定我们看见什么。我不再默认自己的笔刷，或正好流行的风格，就是“它本来的样子”。如果不加追问地跟随算法的口味，我不是在做选择，而是在继承一个选择。

02

The audience is a participant观众是参与者

In Web 1.0 people could only read; in Web 2.0 they comment, share and make their own version. So a piece of visual work is not finished when I export it — it gets finished by the people who respond to it. I design for the response now, not only for the screenshot. Web 1.0 的人只能阅读；Web 2.0 的人会评论、转发，并做出自己的版本。所以一件视觉作品，不是在我导出时就完成的——它由回应它的人完成。我现在为“回应”而设计，而不只是为了那一张好看的截图。

03

Be honest with images对图像保持诚实

Photo editing and AIGC can produce a beautiful picture of something that never happened. That is exactly why the rule stays simple: I cannot fool people with false material. The work can be soft, quiet and emotional — but it cannot be a lie. 修图与 AIGC 可以做出很美、却从未发生过的东西。正因如此，这条规则才要简单：不能用虚假的材料骗人。作品可以柔软、安静、充满情绪——但它不能是谎言。

Where does criticism sit?批评应该站在哪里？

Drag the marker — or pick a name. My answer never settles at either end. 拖动标记，或者点一个名字。我的答案从不停在两端。

my position我的位置

Baudelaire & Zola, 19th century波德莱尔与左拉，19世纪

Only personal taste: “I like it” or “I don't.” No history, no theory, no context. Criticism becomes a mood — and a mood cannot be argued with.只凭个人喜好：“我喜欢”或“我不喜欢”。不谈历史、不谈理论、不谈语境。批评于是变成一种心情——而心情是无法被讨论的。

Diderot, the father of modern art criticism, wrote the Salon reviews from his own point of view. What he published was a personal opinion — and that public, subjective voice became the counter-weight to the royal academy's rigid rules.现代艺术批评之父狄德罗，写沙龙评论时本身就带着自己的视角。他发表的是一种私人意见——而正是这种公众的、主观的声音，成为了制衡皇家学院僵化教条的砝码。

Baudelaire and Zola took sides for the Impressionists before the academy did. Clear positions, not neutrality — criticism as advocacy. It is not objective, and it still changed what counted as art.在学院承认之前，波德莱尔与左拉就主动为印象派站队。是鲜明立场，而非中立——批评成为一种辩护。它并不客观，却确实改变了什么算作艺术。

Formalist criticism built a theoretical frame — flatness, pure colour, the properties of the medium — so that judgement would not collapse back into liking and disliking. Full subjectivity is stopped here, but somebody still chose what to look at.形式主义批评搭起一套理论框架——平面性、色彩的纯粹性、媒介自身的特质——让判断不至于又滑回“喜欢 / 不喜欢”。完全的主观在这里被拦住了，但“看什么”仍然是有人在选。

Institutional critique uses academic theory to examine Eurocentrism and systemic bias inside art institutions. The tools are shared and the evidence matters — but the choice of which wall to look at is still made by a person.机构批判借助学术理论，剖析艺术机构内部的欧洲中心主义与结构性偏见。工具是共享的，证据是重要的——但“去看哪一面墙”，仍然是人做出来的选择。

Good criticism includes the feeling the work produced in me — then puts that feeling on a ground of art history, formal analysis, social context and theory. Subjectivity gives criticism its energy. Objectivity gives it something to argue with. 好的艺术批评，既包含作品在我身上引起的主观感受，也把这份感受安放在艺术史、形式分析、社会语境与理论之上。主观给批评以活力，客观给批评以可讨论的依据。

Ⅰ. The crisis

A lot of art today tries to give simple final answers. Many creators use fixed templates, and push audiences to think in only one way.

Art opinions also go to two bad extremes. Some people judge art only by their own likes. Others follow strict old rules and close off new ideas. Many works look nice and perfect, but lose real personal feelings.

Ⅱ. Our definition

Art carries human feelings, and it should not give orders to people.

Art does not need to tell you what you must think. It turns hard-to-describe feelings into works we can see. Good art keeps the creator's real personal ideas, and also respects real-world background. It is not just random feelings, and there is no single perfect standard for art.

Art shows our inner world, but it cannot fix every problem.

Ⅲ. The commandments

  1. Say no to moral lectures. Do not tell people what is right or wrong. Let conflict, weakness and imperfection exist in art.
  2. Use visuals to show feelings. Pictures and small details can express emotion, not only written words.
  3. Keep your true thoughts, and respect real context. Follow your own feelings, but do not ignore reality and the viewer's experience.
  4. Value common daily experiences. You do not need perfect characters. Small everyday feelings are worth showing in art.
  5. Understand what art cannot do. Art can comfort and inspire us, but it cannot take the place of real-life human connection.

Ⅳ. The future

Stop thinking in simple black-and-white ways when making or judging art! Do not tell others how they should feel. Show how people truly feel. Make art a soft space for different inner voices. Let art see every real human feeling.

Maurizio Cattelan's work Comedian showed up at Art Basel Miami Beach in 2019. I first saw posts about this piece on Xiaohongshu. It is just a normal banana stuck to a white wall with silver duct tape. Bananas go bad quickly, so exhibition staff had to swap them out for fresh ones throughout the show. When I first saw photos of this installation, I immediately thought of Marcel Duchamp's famous readymade Fountain.

People even said the store-bought urinal Duchamp showed might have been one he had used himself. Both artists put ordinary everyday objects inside gallery spaces for visitors to see. Once word got out it sold for around 120,000 US dollars, lots of visitors crowded there just to take selfies. There is no fancy painted work or tricky handmade stuff in this piece. Imagine standing in front of it: all you see is a yellow banana, some silver tape and that plain white wall.

A banana is just cheap food you can grab from any supermarket. Taping it to a wall will not change the fruit in any way. It is being inside a gallery that changes how people look at it. Then performance artist David Datuna pulled the banana off and ate it right on-site. The gallery just put up another banana. No one planned for this real-world moment, yet it became part of this simple exhibit.

The artwork does not hand you one fixed answer. It brings up real-life questions. What even counts as art? It also makes you wonder what makes artworks so expensive. It does not force you to take one side; it just throws these topics out for people to disagree on.

I get why this banana-based exhibit got so many ordinary people talking about modern art. To be honest, I was also confused at first. Still, you will not get much out of it if you know nothing about gallery-related news. A random person might just glance at taped-on banana and miss all these ideas. You really need to know the surrounding story to follow what is going on. It does make regular people think about art's value, but only for those who already know the background context.

How do you think proper art criticism must be done? Entirely subjective? Entirely objective? Or some point in between?

My view is that proper art criticism should lie between pure objectivity and pure subjectivity. It can never be fully objective, nor should it become nothing more than personal preference. Here I think of a popular online meme, "站中间串两边". It jokes about people who shift their positions like loaches and hold unclear stances. However, I believe there is no clear black-and-white line between subjectivity and objectivity. Therefore, I will analyze both sides and support my arguments with the history of art criticism and academia.

Why complete objectivity is impossible

In the pre-modern era, art judgment was controlled by guilds, religions and patrons. Evaluation standards came from craftsmanship, religious education and political demands. These were man-made rules, not unchanging objective truth.

In the 18th century, Denis Diderot, the father of modern art criticism, wrote reviews for Salon exhibitions from his own subjective viewpoint. Public subjective opinions worked as a counter-force against the rigid "objective" rules of royal academies. In the 19th century, rebellious critics such as Baudelaire and Zola spoke up for the avant-garde Impressionists. They held clear personal positions and did not stay neutral or purely objective.

The digital age of the 21st century further proves this point. Social media makes art criticism decentralized and open to the public. Different viewers have different personal experiences. There is no universal, totally objective standard for art.

Why art criticism cannot be fully subjective

Full subjectivity means following only personal taste and ignoring historical context, art theory and art history.

In 20th-century formalist criticism, Clement Greenberg judged artworks based on formal features such as the flatness of canvas, pure colour and media qualities. He built a theoretical framework so that criticism would not turn into random personal likes or dislikes. Later, art criticism adopted theoretical tools including Marxism, psychoanalysis and structuralism. These gave people shared grounds for discussion instead of simple statements of "I like it" or "I do not like it".

Even institutional critique in the 21st century does not depend purely on personal feelings. Critics use academic theories to examine Eurocentrism and systemic bias inside art institutions. Subjective insights need support from theory and historical facts.

Therefore, I think good art criticism includes the critic's personal feelings towards the work. Meanwhile, opinions should be based on art history, formal analysis, social context and relevant theories. Subjectivity gives criticism energy and special insights. "Objectivity", represented by historical facts, evidence and theories, makes criticism something that can be argued with.

Abstract

The preschool stage (ages 3–6) represents a critical window for the development of children's emotional cognition and regulation skills. Emotional-themed picture books have increasingly become a popular category in the children's book market; however, there remains ongoing debate regarding whether ordinary children require specialized art therapy picture books to guide them through daily emotional challenges.

Drawing on theories from child developmental psychology and visual communication, this study employs the original therapeutic picture book 《Little X's Growth Journey》 as a practical case to examine the core question: "Do ordinary children aged 3–6 need specialized art therapy picture books to guide their daily emotions?"

The research findings indicate that specialized art therapy picture books can provide positive auxiliary value for the emotional development of young children — by aligning with their cognitive characteristics, offering emotional mirrors, and facilitating parent-child communication. Nevertheless, such books are not essential for a child's emotional growth and cannot replace the role of authentic parent-child interaction.

This study concludes that the appropriate positioning of high-quality therapeutic picture books should be that of a companion to growth rather than a "cure" for emotions; they should serve as supplementary tools to enhance parent-child emotional communication.

Keywords — preschool children aged 3–6; art therapy picture books; emotional acceptance; children's emotional development; parent-child shared reading

The four sections

  1. During the critical period of emotional development, picture books serve as an ideal guiding medium for young children's cognition.
  2. Provide emotional mirroring to help children achieve identity formation and emotional acceptance.
  3. As a medium for parent-child communication, reduce the didactic tone of emotional conversations.
  4. Refutation of counterarguments: "not essential" is not the same as "valueless", and a healing picture book is not a didactic one.

Reflection 08 — which theory matters most to me

Among Visual Communication, Applied Media Aesthetics and Digital Media Theory, I think Digital Media Theory is the most relevant one for me. First of all, it does not only teach me how to make pretty pictures. If I only learn drawing skills, I will just be a person who knows how to use software. But digital media theory pushes me to think deeper about my works. I start to notice things I used to ignore, like software settings, platform rules and how audiences interact with my creation.

Second, it helps me understand the world we live in now. We are surrounded by digital content every day. I learn that media is not completely neutral. Algorithms, platforms and even default settings in drawing tools can influence what we see and how we think. When I create art or design work, I will keep this in mind. I will not just follow popular styles blindly because of algorithm preferences.

Third, this theory gives me a clear way to look at artworks. I can no longer only judge a piece by whether it looks good. I will also think about its technical background, user experience, and what social messages it carries.

To sum up, visual skills are necessary for my major. But digital media theory gives me critical thinking. It tells me why I create, and what influence my work may bring to other people.

Reflection 12 / 13 — rhetoric in my future work

For my future career path, rhetoric is not merely a skill for persuading people with words. It is also a core thinking tool for digital interactive creation and game content production.

First, rhetoric helps me move beyond personal preferences and build objective thinking for analysis and evaluation. When I work on game PVs, short-video materials and interactive UI design, I can easily fall into subjective judgments like "I think this image looks good". Training in rhetoric teaches me to assess works from the perspectives of system design, interaction mechanics and audience experience.

Second, Aristotle's three rhetorical pillars — Ethos, Pathos and Logos — can be directly applied to my real-world work. Pathos means understanding audience tastes and stirring users' feelings. When I design game UI and interactive interfaces, the rules and feedback loops stand for Logos. Meanwhile, brand materials and consistent IP visuals help build Ethos and keep the game IP trustworthy.

What is more, digital-media rhetoric does not rely only on text. Images, interfaces, interactions and visual materials made by AIGC all send out ideas on their own. When I create game key visuals, co-branding materials or AI comic projects, I will also think about how the whole visual system guides and shapes user experience.
